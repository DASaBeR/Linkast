# Linkast 1.3.1

A much more detailed log, so a stream problem (a drop, a restart, a bitrate that jumps) can be explained from the log file instead of guessed.

## New

- **Numbers every 30 seconds while you stream.** One line in the log shows the encoder's real output bitrate and the bitrate actually sent to the server (each with its lowest and highest value), how much video is waiting to be sent, the frame rate, dropped and duplicated frames, reconnects, and which network or proxy the stream uses.
- **Whether your game still responds.** When you capture a game window, each of those lines says if the game is responding, **not responding**, minimized, or gone. If a game freezes while you stream, the log now shows exactly when.
- **Every change of state is explained.** The log records each step (connecting, live, reconnecting, stopped) and why it happened, for example "the connection was reset by the server" or "FFmpeg exited with code …" with its last messages.
- **A summary when the stream ends:** how long it ran, reconnects, the average, lowest and highest bitrate sent, total data sent, dropped frames and the largest backlog.
- **A "Stream settings" line when you go live:** network or proxy, video and audio settings and microphone level, so a log is understandable on its own.

## Tips

- **Sending us a log:** press **Copy report** (or **Open logs**) in Linkast and send the file. Stream keys are hidden in it.
- **A bitrate that looks jumpy on a graph** is normal for video: the encoder sends a large keyframe every 2 seconds, so a per-second graph is spiky even when the average is on target. The 30-second lines in the log show the average.

## Known limitations

- The limitations of [1.3.0](1.3.0.md) still apply.
